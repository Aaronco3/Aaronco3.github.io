About Me

Hi, I'm Aaron.

Yale ME + Economics. I build things that move — rockets, control systems, and prototype hardware — and I care about the full loop from design to test data.

Background

Engineering through hardware, not just theory.

I grew up in Florida and came to Yale to study Mechanical Engineering and Economics. Most of my project work has sat at the intersection of aerospace systems, hardware testing, controls, and team leadership — the kind of work that requires both technical judgment and clear communication.

At Yale Aerospace, I've worked on propulsion and ground support systems for a liquid rocket program targeting FAR-OUT 2026. Before that, I spent time at Floe working on deicing hardware — iterating on prototype manifolds, running spray tests, and moving designs from concept to field-ready product.

My senior capstone was a valved pulsejet engine: designed, machined, and hot-fire tested over six test campaigns with 79 trials and real combustion data. It's a good example of how I approach work — remote operation, instrumented testing, and fast iteration.

This site isn't a resume. It's a place to show the projects I've cared enough to document fully: what the goal was, how it was built, what the data showed, and what I'd change.
