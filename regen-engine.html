Liquid Rocket Test Infrastructure

Regen Engine

Structures and ground-support work for a student-designed regeneratively cooled liquid bipropellant engine. My work focused on the GSE layout, tank integration, test carriage, structural validation, and pressure-test readiness.

4,450 N theoretical engine thrust
21,700 N-s theoretical total impulse
5,000 lbf test carriage axial load target
FS 5 carriage design factor of safety

Project Goal

The regen engine program targeted a single-pass regeneratively cooled liquid engine using ethane fuel and nitrous oxide oxidizer. The engine required supporting hardware that could safely route fluids, hold tanks, protect operators, and collect thrust and pressure data during testing.

I led structures work in Fall 2024, setting design targets, reviewing team designs, writing change orders, and helping move the system from CAD toward integrated test hardware.

Test Carriage

I owned the test carriage design requirements and validation. The carriage was built around aluminum extrusion, custom gusset plates, stainless 5/16-18 fasteners, and a low-friction thrust path into the load cell.

The design was checked for a 5,000 lbf axial thrust load with a factor of safety of 5. I validated the carriage with ANSYS FEA and used the results to support final design decisions before manufacturing and assembly.

GSE and Integration

The ground support layout combined tank mounting, pressure plumbing, electronics, shielding, and engine access into a mobile test configuration. The system needed to be serviceable during integration while maintaining physical separation between propellant sources and sensitive hardware.

The fluids system was validated through pressure testing before hot-fire operations. This helped verify plumbing integrity, fittings, and system readiness before the engine was run under more demanding conditions.

Validation

The validation work focused on the hardware most likely to drive test risk: the thrust structure and the fluids system. The carriage needed to transfer engine loads into the load cell without excessive compliance or friction, while the pressure system needed to be verified before field operations.

ANSYS FEA

Validated the test carriage structure against thrust loading and checked stress concentrations around joints and plates.

Pressure Testing

Used pressure testing to validate fluids-system integrity before moving into higher-risk engine operations.

Test Data Path

Designed the carriage around a cleaner thrust path into the load cell for improved measurement quality.

Technical Contributions

My work sat at the boundary between structures, fluids, and test operations. I focused on converting engine requirements into test hardware that could be reviewed, assembled, validated, and operated by the team.

Structures Lead

Set design targets, reviewed subsystem designs, and coordinated interfaces across structures, fluids, and electronics.

Tank and GSE Layout

Supported tank mounting, shielding, cart layout, and accessibility for integration and troubleshooting.

Test Readiness

Helped prepare the system for field operations through structural checks, pressure testing, and test-data planning.

Reflection

This project reinforced that propulsion development depends heavily on the hardware around the engine. The carriage, plumbing, shielding, instrumentation, and validation process determine whether the engine can be tested safely and whether the resulting data is useful for design iteration.
