The goal: design, manufacture, and launch a liquid-fueled vehicle to compete at the annual Friends of Amateur Rocketry Oxidizers Unlimited Tournament (FAR-OUT) in June 2026. Subassemblies have completed the design phase and are finalizing manufacturing and component validation.

Structures — Subteam Lead, Spring 2025

Upper Airframe

Constructed from in-house manufactured G10 fiberglass — chosen for its high strength and RF signal transparency, enabling external communication with the GPS locator during recovery. Bulkheads are machined from 1/8" aluminum to withstand parachute loads.

The nosecone is fiberglass, sized to support a 2U cubesat payload per competition regulations. A 4:5 Von Karman Ogive profile optimizes aerodynamic performance.

Upper airframe photo / CAD

Lower Airframe

Constructed from aluminum tubes and bulkheads, designed to resist internal pressures of the fuel and oxidizer tanks, and to serve as detachable clamshells for servicing fluid components.

Avionics Bay — Responsible Engineer, Spring 2025

The avionics bay consists of an internal fiberglass body compressed between two welded bulk plates using internally threaded rods, to which the avionics mounting plates are attached. It serves as the coupler between two external body tubes, compressing a 1" switch band. Two ports equalize internal and external pressure, allowing the altimeter to correctly trigger parachute deployment.

Design Drivers

  • Aerodynamic tension forces along the vehicle axis
  • Volume constraints of recovery hardware
  • Drogue parachute opening loads
