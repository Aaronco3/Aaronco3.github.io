Pulsejet Engine

A senior capstone project focused on designing, manufacturing, and hot-fire testing a custom valved pulsejet engine. The project combined propulsion theory, fluid-system design, controls, data acquisition, safety planning, and repeated test iteration.

Propulsion Design-build-test Hot-fire testing DAQ Fluid systems Controls
6 hot-fire test days
79 total test trials
17.5 N mean thrust measured from impulse
98 Hz dominant resonant combustion frequency

Project Goal

The goal was to create a low-cost, low-part-count propulsion platform that could be designed, built, and tested by an undergraduate team. Pulsejets are useful for this kind of project because they avoid the compressors and turbines of conventional jet engines while still requiring real combustion, controls, instrumentation, and safety work.

Our team focused on three goals: remote engine operation, engine characterization, and analysis of cyclic pulsing behavior.

System Design

The final system included a steel jet tube, custom reed-valve intake, formed air duct, forced-air startup source, gasoline feed system, remote switchbox, spark ignition, thrust stand, and sensor package. The engine geometry controlled the resonant behavior, while the reed valve worked as a high-frequency check valve that admitted air during the intake phase and closed during combustion.

Jet Tube

Steel chamber, converging section, and exhaust tube sized around resonant combustion behavior.

Reed Valve

Thin metal reeds deflected open under intake flow and closed during combustion.

Fuel + Controls

Pump, bypass regulator, solenoid valve, spark plug, relays, and remote switchbox enabled safe testing.

Reed Valve + Airflow

The reed valve was one of the most important design elements. We tested different reed materials, reed lengths, deflection limiters, and duct geometries to increase airflow while keeping the valve responsive at the expected operating frequency.

Hotwire anemometer testing validated the final valve and duct at approximately 75 g/s of airflow, meeting the requirement for the hot-fire campaign. The final reed design had an estimated natural frequency of 328 Hz, above the measured operating frequency of the engine.

Manufacturing

Early fit checks were built with PLA prototypes before moving to final metal parts. The reed valve components were water-jetted and welded, duct walls were stamped using 3D-printed molds, and the jet tube was fabricated from stock steel tubing. Support hardware used steel near the hot engine and aluminum 8020 for the surrounding test structure.

Testing

Testing was built around an EHS-reviewed procedure with defined operator roles, a test card, hazards and mitigations, and a clear-zone process. The setup was designed for fast turnaround between trials while keeping fuel, ignition, forced air, and data collection controlled from a safe distance.

Remote operation

Fuel pump, solenoid valve, spark ignition, and air startup were controlled away from the engine.

High-speed DAQ

Thrust, chamber pressure, chamber temperature, and valve state were recorded at 2500 Hz.

Rapid iteration

Test turnaround improved from about 30 minutes to about 2 minutes across the campaign.

Results

The engine achieved pulsed combustion during hot-fire testing. Load cell data showed a clear periodic thrust signature, with a time-averaged thrust of approximately 17.5 N and peak pulse amplitudes near 50 N.

Frequency analysis of load cell and audio data showed a dominant peak at 98 Hz, identifying the engine’s fundamental operating frequency. Additional harmonic content suggested a sharp-rise, slow-decay pulse shape, matching expected valved pulsejet behavior.

Reflection

This project showed me that propulsion hardware is never just the engine. The test stand, safety procedure, instrumentation, controls, and iteration loop matter just as much as the combustion hardware. Small changes in reed material, fuel flow, tube length, and startup airflow could determine whether the engine pulsed cleanly, burned without resonance, or failed to sustain combustion.

The most valuable part was seeing a custom mechanical system move from theory and CAD into a real, measured test campaign.
